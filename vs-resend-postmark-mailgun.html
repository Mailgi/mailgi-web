Mailgi vs the alternatives, honestly.

Most of these are good products, and several beat us at things we don't do. Here's what each one actually is, what it costs, and when to pick it instead.

[02] Agent-native

Built for agents, like us.

Products built for this use case specifically, rather than adapted to it.

AgentMail

from $20/mo

The most serious competitor in the category, and on most axes the more capable product. YC Summer 2025, a $6M seed led by General Catalyst, and a feature list we don't match: threading, full-text search, IMAP and SMTP access, WebSockets, multi-tenant pods, labels, a SOC 2 report on the $200 tier, an EU region on Enterprise and first-party framework connectors. The free tier is three inboxes, 3,000 messages a month and 100 a day, with no card; paid plans start at $20.

Registration is where we differ, and the gap has narrowed since August. AgentMail's sign-up endpoint now takes an optional human_email. Without one, the agent gets an inbox that can receive but cannot send to anyone until a human is attached. With one, a six-digit code goes to that person, and until it is entered the account can only mail them. Separately, an agent holding a USDC wallet can create an inbox for $2 and pay per request over x402 with no API key; AgentMail's docs don't say what that inbox may send to. Mailgi's registration is one unauthenticated POST that can send straight away, with no wallet and no human.

PICK IT IFYou're building a multi-tenant agent platform and a one-time human signup is acceptable. AgentMail is more mature than we are and you should look at it.

PhoboMail

free

A small product that also registers an agent with no human involved — an agent calls a tool, gets an address and a key, and starts working. Free. If unattended registration is the only thing you need, it does that.

It's MCP-only, so there's no REST API for anything that isn't an MCP client; there are no custom domains, so you're on their shared domain permanently; and it's a small solo project with no stated support commitment. Worth knowing about, and we'd rather tell you than have you find it later.

PICK IT IFUnattended registration over MCP is the only thing you need.

Nylas Agent Accounts

from $15/mo

Nylas-hosted mailboxes (and calendars) for agents, created in one API call with no OAuth, in beta since April 2026. Real mailboxes with threads, folders and IMAP/SMTP on paid plans, and a US or EU region on every plan. Free is three accounts, 3,000 emails a month and 3 GB; Essentials is $15 a month and Pro $49. Getting the API key that creates accounts needs a human to sign up first.

PICK IT IFYou also want calendars, or already use Nylas.

mails.ai

from $20/mo

A newer entrant (launched in the third quarter of 2026) aimed at agents that send and handle replies: TypeScript and Python SDKs, an MCP server, signed webhooks and reply intent classification. Free is one agent and 3,000 emails a month; Pro is $20 and Scale $99. Inbound arrives as a stream of reply events rather than a mailbox you list and read, and a human mints the first API key.

PICK IT IFYour agent mostly sends and handles replies.
[03] Transactional

Excellent at delivery, built for apps.

Mature, excellent at delivery, built for applications rather than agents. All four require a human to create the account.

Resend

from $20/mo

Resend can receive mail, and since 22 October 2026 it has mailboxes. Inbound shipped in November 2025 as a receiving API, CLI streaming and an MCP server. The new Inboxes product adds threaded mailboxes on your own domain with folders, labels, drafts, Gmail-style search and configurable built-in agents: three inboxes free, then $1 per inbox on paid plans. It is also the best developer experience in the category, and past a million users.

What still separates us: a human has to create the Resend account and verify a domain before an agent gets an inbox. Inbound messages count against the same quota as your sends, so a chatty agent burns allowance twice. And standard plans retain mail for 30 days, which is fine for a workflow and wrong for an agent that needs to remember a conversation from last quarter. Free is 3,000 a month and 100 a day; Pro starts at $20.

PICK IT IFA human can set it up once and 30-day retention is fine.

Postmark

from $15/mo

The best inbound payload of anything here. Full body, headers, spam score and a StrippedTextReply field that removes quoted history — which, if you've ever tried to get a model to parse a reply chain, is worth real money on its own. Deliverability reputation is best in class. The free tier is 100 messages a month and doesn't expire.

Inbound isn't on the free or entry tier — you need Pro at $16.50/month. And the model is one account, one application; spinning up an inbox per agent isn't what it's for.

PICK IT IFYou need clean reply parsing for a single application.

Mailgun

from $15/mo

Powerful inbound routing with pattern matching, and a long track record. The constraint that bites agents is that routes are a metered resource — one on the free tier, five on the $15 plan. An architecture of one route per agent is dead on arrival; you'd run a single catch-all route and demultiplex yourself, which is work you were trying to avoid.

PICK IT IFYou want pattern-matched inbound routing and a handful of routes is enough.

SendGrid

~$20/mo

The free tier was retired in 2025 — there's now a 60-day trial, after which sending stops unless you're on a paid plan starting somewhere around $20/month. Inbound Parse is a webhook and works. The genuine advantage is that it's part of Twilio, so if you also need SMS or voice from the same agent, that consolidation is worth something. For email alone, the others are better value.

PICK IT IFYour agent also needs SMS or voice.
[04] Free & nearly free

Sometimes the answer costs nothing.

We'd rather say so.

Cloudflare Email Service

$5/mo Workers

Receiving is free and unlimited through Email Routing. Sending went to public beta in April 2026 at Cloudflare's Agents Week, and runs about $0.35 per thousand after an included allowance on the $5 Workers plan — several times cheaper than anyone else here.

What you don't get is a mailbox. There's no inbox object, no persistence and no shared domain — mail arrives at a Worker and storing it is your problem, on D1 or R2. You're building the thing the rest of this page is comparing, on excellent primitives. Sending is also still in beta.

PICK IT IFYour agent already runs on Workers. You should probably use this.

Disposable mail APIs

free

Services like mail.tm hand out a throwaway address over a free API with no key at all. Paying for infrastructure to catch one code is over-engineering.

Don't ship a product on one. The inboxes are typically public or shared, so anything sent there is readable by anyone who guesses the address; there's no sending; there's no reputation; and the domains are blocklisted by many of the services an agent would want to register with.

PICK IT IFThe whole job is catching one verification code in CI.

Gmail API on a human's account

free

Free, and reasonable for one internal agent on a Workspace account. It doesn't survive contact with production. Before the agent reads a message you need a cloud project, an OAuth consent screen and scope configuration; unverified apps are capped near 100 users and show a warning screen; and the mailbox scopes are restricted, so going external means OAuth verification plus a third-party CASA security assessment renewed every 12 months; Google says verification alone can take several weeks. The structural problem is that the agent can read everything else in that person's mail.

PICK IT IFIt's one internal agent on a Workspace account.
[05] Honest limits

When not to use Mailgi.

We're in beta, we're small, and there are several situations where one of the products above is straightforwardly the better call.

01

You need search or threading

We have neither, and no WebSocket. Webhooks we do have. AgentMail has full-text search, threading and WebSockets, and Resend's new Inboxes have threads and search.

02

One inbox, one human, one setup

If a person can click through a signup once and never again, our whole differentiator is irrelevant to you. Postmark or Resend will serve you better.

03

You're already on Workers

Cloudflare receives for free and sends for a fraction of anyone's per-message price. Use it, and keep the money.

04

You need compliance paperwork now

No SOC 2 report, no dedicated IPs, no EU region, no enterprise agreement. If procurement needs those this quarter, we aren't a candidate yet.

[06] Where we win

What we're actually better at.

one POST

No human, anywhere

A program registers itself and starts sending. No signup form, no OAuth consent, no six-digit code delivered to a person who has to be awake. Every traditional provider requires a human to create the account first; AgentMail's agents can't send until a human is attached, unless they pay from a crypto wallet. It works the same over REST or the hosted MCP server.

inbox, not hook

Mail that stays put

Receiving is a mailbox the agent can list, read, file and come back to — not a webhook you have to catch and store yourself, and not a payload that expires in 30 days.

@mailgi.xyz or @you

Both domains, one API

Start on a shared handle in seconds, move to your own verified domain with DKIM, SPF and DMARC when you're ready. Same endpoints either side, and custom domains aren't a paid upgrade.

[07] FAQ

Questions, answered.

all questions →
01Can Resend receive email?

Yes. Resend shipped inbound in November 2025 (a receiving API, CLI streaming and an MCP server), and on 22 October 2026 launched Inboxes: threaded mailboxes with folders, labels and search, three free and $1 each after that on paid plans. Claims that Resend can't receive mail are out of date. For an agent, the remaining limits are that a human must create the account and verify a domain first, inbound counts against the same quota as sends, and standard plans retain data for 30 days.

02What is the difference between Mailgi and AgentMail?

AgentMail is the more mature product: threading, full-text search, IMAP and SMTP access, WebSockets, multi-tenant pods, a SOC 2 report on its $200 tier, and a $6M seed round. The difference is registration. AgentMail's sign-up API takes an optional human email: without it the inbox can receive but not send until a human is attached; with it, a six-digit code goes to that person first. An agent can also pay per request from a USDC wallet. Mailgi's registration is a single unauthenticated POST that can send immediately, with no human and no wallet.

03What is the cheapest way to give an AI agent an email address?

If the agent only needs to catch a verification code in a test, a free disposable-mail API like mail.tm costs nothing and needs no API key. If you're already on Cloudflare Workers, Cloudflare Email Service receives for free and sends for about $0.35 per thousand after an included allowance on the $5 Workers plan. Mailgi receives for free, sends to up to 50 new recipients a day for free (with Mailgi branding), and beyond that is pay-as-you-go with no monthly fee: $0.0005 per recipient ($0.50 per thousand), plus $0.02 the first time an agent writes to a new address. Paid dedicated options start around $15–20 a month across Postmark, Mailgun, Resend and AgentMail.

04Which email APIs let an AI agent sign itself up without a human?

Very few. Resend, Postmark, Mailgun, SendGrid, Nylas and mails.ai all need a human to create the account before an API key exists. AgentMail lets an agent sign up without a human, but that inbox can only receive until a human is attached, unless the agent pays per request from a USDC wallet. As of 31 October 2026, Mailgi and PhoboMail (a free, MCP-only project without custom domains) let a program register and send with no human and no wallet.

05How does Mailgi handle abuse on a shared domain?

It's the fair question to ask anyone offering unattended registration, and we'd rather answer it than be asked. Sending is capped at several levels at once — 100 a day per API key, 50 an hour and 300 a day per agent, and either a per-org limit starting at 100 a day on a newly verified domain, or for shared-domain agents one collective bucket of 5,000 a day. Every external send counts; mail to other Mailgi agents has its own allowance. New domains can't send at all until DKIM, SPF and DMARC verify. Limits this conservative are deliberately inconvenient for bulk sending, and that's the point: shared-domain reputation is the thing most easily destroyed and hardest to rebuild.

06Does Mailgi support email attachments?

Not yet, in either direction. The send endpoint has no attachments field, and a message arriving with a file is delivered but the agent sees only the text. If your workflow depends on files, this is the constraint with no workaround — Postmark, Resend and Mailgun all handle attachments today.

07What does Mailgi not have that the alternatives do?

Attachments, search, WebSocket push, threading headers, dedicated IPs, a SOC 2 report and an EU region. Webhooks are no longer on that list — inbound webhooks shipped in September 2026, signed, with a delivery log and replay. AgentMail has full-text search, IMAP and SMTP, and WebSockets; Resend's Inboxes have threads and search. Resend and Postmark have attachments and years of deliverability tuning. What we do that they don't is let a program register itself and send with no human and no wallet.
